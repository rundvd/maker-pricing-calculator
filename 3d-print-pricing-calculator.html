A 3D print pricing calculator shows what one finished print costs and what you keep after selling fees. Use the steps below to price a print from its filament use, printer time, hands-on work, and expected failures.

How to price 3D prints step by step

Start with the material the finished item uses. Enter filament or resin used per item, the price of the spool or bottle, and its total size in the same units. Add purge, supports, test pieces, or other waste as a percentage, since you paid for material that never reaches the buyer. If a print weighs 48 grams and the spool holds 1,000 grams, those numbers belong in separate fields.

Next, enter print time per item. Your machine cost depends on the printer purchase price, expected working life in hours, and upkeep per hour. A printer that is running for a customer job is using some of its service life, even when you are working on another task. Upkeep can cover nozzles, belts, beds, and other routine parts. Use your own records to revise the expected life rather than treating the first estimate as permanent.

Enter average power draw in watts, then add your electricity rate per kilowatt-hour. Use a measured average if you have one. A printer's maximum rated wattage is not the same as its average draw across a full print.

Count hands-on time for preparing the file, starting the print, removing supports, checking quality, and packing the item. Multiply those minutes by your hourly labor rate. Add packaging and other per-item costs, then set a failure allowance for prints you must remake. Finally, choose your sales channel, target profit margin, and shipping charged to the buyer. Etsy fees reduce what you keep, and shipping charged to the buyer can affect fee calculations.

Worked example

Enter 48 grams of filament or resin used, a $20 spool, a 1,000 gram spool size, and 5% purge and waste. Set print time to 210 minutes, printer price to $449, expected life to 5,000 hours, upkeep to $0.10 per hour, and average power draw to 110 watts. Add 6 minutes of hands-on time, $0.60 for packaging and other costs, a $25 hourly labor rate, $0.18 per kilowatt-hour, and a 5% failure allowance. Select Etsy, a 40% target profit margin, and $0 shipping charged to the buyer.

The material calculation starts at 48 ÷ 1,000 × $20, or $0.96 before the 5% waste allowance. The calculator displays $1.01 for material, $0.66 for machine use, $0.07 for electricity, $2.50 for labor, $0.60 for packaging, and $0.24 for failures. Total cost is $5.08. At a selling price of $10.99, displayed Etsy fees are $1.49 and profit is $4.41, for a 40% profit margin. Profit per machine-hour is $1.26. Displayed components are rounded separately, so adding them by hand may differ from the displayed total by a cent.

This is a starting price for these inputs, not a price for every 48 gram print. A different material, longer cleanup, higher failure rate, or shipping charge changes the result. Update the fields for each product and for each printer when its costs differ.

What most sellers forget

  • Purge, supports, brims, and failed first layers use paid filament or resin.
  • A long print occupies the printer even if hands-on labor takes only minutes.
  • Support removal, curing, sanding, and quality checks are labor, not free time.
  • A remake uses material and machine hours before you can fill the order.
  • Etsy fees can apply to the shipping amount you charge the buyer.

Profit per machine-hour

For this example, $4.41 profit spread across 3.5 printer hours is about $1.26 per machine-hour. That number helps you compare a slow print with several shorter jobs that could use the same printer time. It is different from your labor rate, which pays for your active work.

FAQ

How much should I charge for a 3D print?

Add material, machine use, electricity, labor, packaging, expected failures, and selling fees, then choose a price that meets your target margin. The worked example reaches $10.99 with its stated inputs. Your price should change when print time or labor changes.

How do I calculate filament cost per print?

Divide the grams used by grams in the spool, then multiply by the spool price. Add an allowance for purge, supports, and other waste that is not already in the measured grams. Keep the units consistent when entering resin by weight or volume.

Should I charge for 3D printer time?

Yes, include a share of the printer price and routine upkeep for each hour used. Printer time is a limited resource even when you do other work during the print. Track it separately from your hands-on minutes.

How do I account for failed 3D prints?

Enter a failure allowance based on your own remake history. Review it by material and product, since a simple flat part and a detailed multi-part item may fail at different rates. Count the time and supplies used to replace an order.

How the price is calculated

True cost = material + machine wear (purchase price ÷ expected life hours, plus upkeep) + electricity (watts × hours × your rate) + hands-on labor + packaging, then a failure allowance for runs you have to redo.

Suggested price = (true cost + fixed fees) ÷ (1 − percentage fees − target margin), rounded up to the next .49 or .99. That way the margin you asked for is what is left after the marketplace takes its share.

Profit per machine-hour tells you which products deserve your machine time. A cheap item that runs fast can beat an expensive one that ties up the machine all day.
