A resin pricing calculator shows what one finished piece really costs and what you keep after selling fees. It works for coasters, trays, keychains, bookmarks and boards. Use the steps below to price your own pieces, including the molds that wear out and the pours that don't make it.

How to price resin step by step

Start with the resin in one finished item, part A and part B mixed. Weigh a test pour or use the mold's volume: cubic inches times 0.554 gives fluid ounces. Then enter what your resin kit costs and how many ounces it holds, so the calculator can work out cost per ounce. Round your amount up a little, because some resin always stays in the cup and on the stir stick.

Add mica, alcohol ink, glitter, dried flowers and anything else you pour in. Then add the blanks and hardware the piece needs: cork dots, keychain rings, tassels, handles or a wood blank.

Molds don't last forever. Enter the mold's price and the number of pours you expect from it, and the calculator spreads that cost over each pour. A $19.99 coaster mold that lasts about 40 pours adds about $0.50 each time you fill it.

Enter your hands-on minutes for one item: mixing, pouring, torching bubbles, demolding, sanding the edges, coating and packing. Waiting for a cure isn't hands-on time. Multiply by the hourly rate you want to pay yourself.

Then add a failure allowance. Bubbles, soft cures, warped pieces and dust in the surface all cost resin and supplies, and every piece you sell has to cover its share. Pick a sales channel and a target margin, and Etsy fees come out of the price automatically.

Worked example

Enter 7.5 oz of mixed resin for a set of four 4 in coasters, a $79.99 kit that holds 128 oz, $0.80 for mica and inclusions, a $19.99 mold that lasts 40 pours and $0.50 for cork and hardware. Add 25 minutes of hands-on time and $1.10 for a gift box. Set your labor rate to $25 an hour, the failure allowance to 7%, the channel to Etsy and the target margin to 40%. Leave shipping and your price at $0.

The resin costs about $4.69 and the mold adds about $0.50, so displayed material cost is $6.49. The calculator shows $10.42 for labor, $1.10 for packaging and $1.26 for failed pours, so the true cost of one set is $19.26.

The suggested price is $39.49. At that price, Etsy fees are $4.20, profit is $16.02 and margin is 41%. The calculator rounds what it displays, so adding up the rounded figures by hand can be off by a cent.

What most sellers forget

  • Resin left in the cup and on the stir stick is resin you paid for.
  • Gloves, mixing cups, tape and sandpaper are small, but you use them on every pour.
  • Molds tear and cloud. Spread their price over the pours they actually last.
  • A failed pour wastes the resin and the mold's wear, not just your evening.
  • Sanding and edge finishing often take longer than the pour itself. Time them once.
  • Gift boxes and padding for shipping belong in the cost of every set.
  • Etsy charges fees on shipping you collect, not just on the piece.

Your real hourly pay

Add profit and labor pay, then divide by your hands-on hours to see what one sale pays you for the time you spent. In the example, $16.02 profit plus $10.42 labor pay over 25 minutes gives about $63.46 an hour, using the calculator's unrounded values. It's a handy way to compare quick keychains with a tray that needs an hour of sanding. It doesn't mean every hour in your business earns that much.

FAQ

How much should I charge for resin coasters?

Add resin, color, mold wear, cork, packaging, labor, failed pours and selling fees before you choose a margin. The worked example suggests $39.49 for a set of four sold on Etsy. Thicker coasters, extra layers or a fancier box need a new calculation.

How do I work out how much resin a mold uses?

Multiply the mold's length, width and depth in inches to get cubic inches, then multiply by 0.554 for fluid ounces. A 4 in round coaster 1/4 in thick holds about 1.75 oz. Add a little for what stays in the cup.

Should I charge more for custom resin orders?

Usually, yes. Custom colors, names and inclusions take extra design and hands-on time, and a one-off pour can't share setup with a batch. Price the extra minutes and any design time, or add a design fee.

Can I sell resin trays for food?

Check your resin maker's food-contact guidance before you sell anything for food. Many makers sell trays and boards for dry foods or decor only and finish wood with a food-safe oil.

How the price is calculated

True cost = material + machine wear (purchase price ÷ expected life hours, plus upkeep) + electricity (watts × hours × your rate) + hands-on labor + packaging, then a failure allowance for runs you have to redo.

Suggested price = (true cost + fixed fees) ÷ (1 − percentage fees − target margin), rounded up to the next .49 or .99. That way the margin you asked for is what is left after the marketplace takes its share.

Profit per machine-hour tells you which products deserve your machine time. A cheap item that runs fast can beat an expensive one that ties up the machine all day.
