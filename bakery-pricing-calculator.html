A bakery pricing calculator shows the cost of one sellable unit from a batch and the profit left after fees. Use it to price cookies by the dozen, cakes, and other baked goods with the time and ingredients each order actually uses.

How to price baked goods step by step

Start with ingredients for a full batch and the number of units that batch makes. Define a unit before entering numbers: one cake, one box of a dozen cookies, or one loaf. Divide batch ingredients by sellable units, not by the number you hoped the recipe would produce. Add packaging per unit, including a box, board, liner, label, or food-safe wrap.

Enter oven minutes per unit. For shared oven time, divide the bake across the units that occupy that oven run. Add the oven purchase price, expected working life in hours, and upkeep per hour to account for equipment use. Average oven draw and your electricity rate estimate power cost. If you use a rented kitchen, include its allocated cost in your broader estimate too.

Next, enter hands-on time per unit and your hourly labor rate. Count mixing, shaping, decorating, cleaning, boxing, and order messages that belong to that item. Add any other packaging or unit cost, then a failure allowance for damaged decorations, broken cookies, or a batch you must remake. A custom cake can have much more active time than a repeat cookie order even when the ingredient bill is similar.

Choose the sales channel, target profit margin, and shipping charged to the buyer. Etsy fees reduce your proceeds if you sell there. Check your local cottage food rules before selling from a home kitchen because permitted foods, labeling, and sales channels vary by location. This guide is a pricing tool, not legal advice.

Worked example

Enter $13 in ingredients per batch and 3 units per batch. Add $1.20 in packaging per unit, 13 minutes of oven time per unit, a $900 oven, 10,000 hours of expected life, $0.03 in upkeep per hour, and 2,000 watts of average oven draw. Enter 67 minutes of hands-on time per unit, $0.15 for packaging and other costs, a $20 hourly labor rate, and electricity at $0.18 per kilowatt-hour. Set failure allowance to 5%, sales channel to Etsy, target profit margin to 30%, shipping charged to buyer to $0, and optional price to $0.

Ingredients cost $13 divided by 3, or about $4.33 per unit. With the $1.20 packaging entry, displayed material cost is $5.53. The calculator shows $0.03 for machine use, $0.08 for electricity, $22.33 for labor, $0.15 for other packaging, and $1.41 for failures. Total cost is $29.53. The suggested price is $49.99, with $5.20 in Etsy fees, $15.26 in profit, a 31% margin, and $33.67 in displayed hourly pay. Displayed components are rounded separately, so their hand-added total can differ by a cent.

What most sellers forget

  • A dozen cookies is one unit only when the batch count is in dozens too.
  • Cake boards, inserts, and protective boxes can cost more than a plain bag.
  • Decorating, cleanup, and customer messages take paid working time.
  • Broken cookies and failed batches reduce the number of sellable units.
  • Oven time, power, and wear belong in the cost even if each amount is small.
  • Etsy fees may also apply to shipping charged to the buyer.

Your real hourly pay

Use (profit + labor pay) divided by hours of hands-on work. In this example, $15.26 profit plus $22.33 labor pay over 67 minutes gives the calculator's displayed $33.67 per hour. The figure helps compare a detailed cake with a repeat batch of cookies; it only reflects the time and costs you entered.

FAQ

How do I price cookies by the dozen?

Treat one dozen as one unit, then divide a batch's ingredients by the number of sellable dozens. Add one box or bag per dozen and the labor to portion, bake, cool, and pack it. Include any broken cookies in your failure allowance.

How much should I charge for a custom cake?

Calculate the ingredients, board and box, oven use, electricity, decoration supplies, and all active design and decorating time for that cake. Custom work often takes more labor than a standard cake of the same size. Recalculate for each design rather than using only a price per serving.

Should oven time count as labor?

Count oven time for equipment and electricity. Count only the minutes you actively work on that order as labor; you can work on another task during an unattended bake. If you must watch or rotate the item, include those active minutes.

Can I sell home-baked goods online?

Rules depend on where you make and sell the food and on the type of product. Check the current cottage food requirements for your state and local area before choosing a sales channel or shipping plan. Pricing does not replace those requirements.

How the price is calculated

True cost = material + machine wear (purchase price ÷ expected life hours, plus upkeep) + electricity (watts × hours × your rate) + hands-on labor + packaging, then a failure allowance for runs you have to redo.

Suggested price = (true cost + fixed fees) ÷ (1 − percentage fees − target margin), rounded up to the next .49 or .99. That way the margin you asked for is what is left after the marketplace takes its share.

Profit per machine-hour tells you which products deserve your machine time. A cheap item that runs fast can beat an expensive one that ties up the machine all day.
