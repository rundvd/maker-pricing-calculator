An embroidery pricing calculator shows the cost of the blank, stitch work, and the time you spend preparing and finishing each item. Use this guide to set a price that covers those costs and the fees taken from an Etsy sale.

How to price embroidery step by step

Start with the blank you will embroider, such as a cap, shirt, or towel. Enter its cost for one usable item. A customer-provided blank may have no purchase cost to you, but a replacement policy and the risk of damage still need a decision before you quote the job. For your own stock, use the real purchase cost rather than the retail price of a similar blank.

Enter the stitch count and your thread cost in the calculator's thread field. Stitch count helps capture how much thread the design uses. Add stabilizer and backing for each item, including layers you trim away after sewing. Change these inputs when a dense fill design replaces a light outline or when a stretchy blank needs different support.

Next, enter sew time per item, machine purchase price, expected machine life in hours, and upkeep per hour. This spreads the equipment cost over its useful sewing time and includes routine service. Enter average power draw and your electricity rate after that. A low electricity line does not mean the machine has no cost; depreciation and upkeep are separate.

Count hands-on time for hooping, placement, thread changes, trimming, cleanup, inspection, and packing. Enter your labor rate and per-item packaging cost. Add a failure allowance for misalignment, broken thread, damaged blanks, or items that fail inspection. Then select Etsy or another sales channel, your target profit margin, and any shipping charged to the buyer. The final price must cover fees after the sale, not just the sewing cost.

Worked example

Enter a $6.50 blank, 8,000 stitches, $0.04 thread cost, and $0.30 of stabilizer and backing. Set sew time to 13 minutes, machine price to $1,500, expected life to 5,000 hours, upkeep to $0.20 per hour, and average draw to 90 watts. Add 8 minutes of hands-on time, $0.60 for packaging and other costs, a $25 hourly labor rate, electricity at $0.18 per kilowatt-hour, and a 3% failure allowance. Choose Etsy, a 40% target margin, and $0 shipping charged to the buyer.

The calculator displays $7.12 for materials, $0.11 for machine use, $0.00 for electricity after rounding, $3.33 for labor, $0.60 for packaging, and $0.33 for failures. Total displayed cost is $11.50. A $23.99 selling price produces displayed Etsy fees of $2.73 and profit of $9.76. The displayed margin is 41%, and profit per machine-hour is $45.05. The $0.00 electricity display means less than one cent at this precision, not that the machine uses no power.

The 40% target and 41% result differ because the selected price is rounded to a selling amount. A larger design, slower sew speed, or pricier blank can change the result quickly. Rework the estimate when the customer changes the garment or design size.

What most sellers forget

  • Stabilizer, topping, backing, and wasted thread add up across a batch.
  • Hooping and placement may take longer than the actual sew time.
  • A damaged customer garment can be harder to replace than your own blank.
  • Thread breaks and rejected stitches may require extra labor or a new blank.
  • Etsy fees can apply to the shipping amount charged to the buyer.

Profit per machine-hour

At 13 minutes of sewing time, the example's $9.76 profit equals $45.05 per machine-hour after rounding. This helps you compare a small embroidered cap with a dense design that ties up the machine longer. Keep hands-on labor in the cost calculation as well, since a high machine-hour figure does not erase hooping and finishing work.

FAQ

How do I price embroidery by stitch count?

Use stitch count to estimate thread use, then add the blank, backing, machine time, labor, packaging, failures, and selling fees. Stitch count alone misses hooping and cleanup. Two 8,000 stitch designs can still take different hands-on time.

How much should I charge for an embroidered shirt?

Use the actual shirt cost and the specific design's stitch count and sew time. The worked example reaches $23.99 with a $6.50 blank and 8,000 stitches. Change the inputs for the shirt and design you will deliver.

Should embroidery setup be included in labor?

Yes, include placement, hooping, thread changes, trimming, and packing in hands-on minutes. Spread a shared setup across a batch when the same design runs repeatedly. Track one-off design preparation separately if it takes extra time.

Why does embroidery profit change with blank cost?

The blank is often a large part of the order's direct cost. When it costs more, the price needed to protect your margin rises too. Recheck the estimate before swapping a standard shirt for a premium one.

How the price is calculated

True cost = material + machine wear (purchase price ÷ expected life hours, plus upkeep) + electricity (watts × hours × your rate) + hands-on labor + packaging, then a failure allowance for runs you have to redo.

Suggested price = (true cost + fixed fees) ÷ (1 − percentage fees − target margin), rounded up to the next .49 or .99. That way the margin you asked for is what is left after the marketplace takes its share.

Profit per machine-hour tells you which products deserve your machine time. A cheap item that runs fast can beat an expensive one that ties up the machine all day.
